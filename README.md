# Triage

Triage is one decision inbox for scheduled jobs and for omp and dsh sessions. It shows one card at a time in priority order. Ask cards show the STAR situation, task, action, and result with options suited to the situation. Skip and later are built in.

## Agent sessions

Triage polls tmux once a second and reads each omp pane's transcript when its title changes to asking. Omp 18.18.7 uses `ask.arguments.html` and returns `details.answers` in form order. Triage converts supported radios, single selects, lone checkboxes, and text fields into native cards, then answers through field controls. Multi-select and multi-checkbox controls stay read-only and must be answered in the pane. Legacy `ask.arguments.questions` and `details.selectedOptions` remain supported. Agents need no triage-side code or plugin.

A reply keeps one card when tmux restarts and omp resumes it under a new pane id: a reply already dealt with stays handled, and a reply whose card is still queued from the old pane moves to the new pane. The tracker trusts a pane's omp breadcrumb only when it was written after the pane's pty appeared, or when the pane's omp was started with `--resume` or `--restore` of that session.
The inbox holds cards that need a decision. An omp run that stops earns an information card. omp shows the same waiting title for a finished run, an interrupted one, and one waiting on background jobs. Triage reads the last assistant message's stop reason. A finished reply earns a card, while errors earn a `Stopped with error` card with an error label and interrupts earn none. A run whose last message is a tool call, whose working row shows a background-job wait or automatic retry, or whose reply comes before a delivered background-job result or supervised-process exit has not stopped yet. Its card comes when it does. omp redraws the working row on every spinner tick. Triage reads three captures before queuing a card. omp drops the wait row when a background job finishes, seconds before the result reaches the transcript. A reply last seen with the row stays held until the transcript changes or 30 seconds pass without the row. A reply counts as a final report when its first line is a status line such as `ENVOY_STATUS: complete`, `ENVOY_STATUS: agent-done`, or `ENVOY_STATUS: incomplete`. Bold, code ticks or a heading mark around that line still count, and text after the status word on the same line starts the report. Every triage brief requires one. Every brief tells the session to finish every step its instructions allow, such as the checks, commit, push or deploy, before it reports. It then asks for a caveman-style report of four short lines at most, with the outcome first so it becomes the card title. The report ends in a `Recommended action:` line that names the one change the session was not allowed to make and the user must approve, or reads `Recommended action: none`. Work waiting on a later event and steps only the user can take also read `none`. Briefs define `complete` as work released through the project's normal pipeline with its full test gate passed. Envoys report `agent-done` when they finish their work but a required push, deploy, or full test gate remains open. Each report names the open step. A final report written before a background wait stays the card's report, and the closing reply follows it in the body. A reply without a status line also ends the run, unless omp will start the session's next turn itself. That is the case while the session owns a supervised process (omp `bash` with `name`) that was started without a readiness gate less than two hours ago, or that exited less than a minute ago and whose exit omp has not yet reported. It is also the case for up to 30 seconds after the transcript last changed while the session's goal is active. A server started with a readiness gate never holds a card. A final reply never waits. A reply that ends the run earns the same card showing its first line as the title and the reply as its body. Reports with no status line have no outcome label. A pane with no assistant message yet makes no card. A newer card of the same session replaces an older one still queued. Triage removes `Recommended action: none` and empty labels. It moves recommendations under Action on investigations and execution reports with status `agent-done` or `incomplete`. An `agent-done` report that closes with `Recommended action: none` names no open step. Its card reads `Done`. Complete reports from other launches keep recommendations in the body. Acknowledge stays the primary action. A report whose recommended step is open offers `Plan next step`, which opens Dispatch preselected to Plan, beside `Do next step`, which opens it preselected to Act. Either one runs in a window of its own, and nothing is typed into the session that just finished. A reply without a status line can show both `Plan next step` and `Request final report`. Carried replies follow the same status and launch-mode rule. Ended-session warnings and ingested `triage.info` bodies still move steps under Action. Finding cards show their Action and options. Ask cards show their choices. Plan cards show their proposals. Error cards keep their diagnostic text.
omp drops the background-job wait row when the job finishes, seconds before its result reaches the transcript. Triage holds any reply last seen with that row for up to 30 seconds while the transcript stays unchanged.
Resuming a session in its pane closes its queued and deferred report cards as `resumed in pane` when its title changes from waiting to working or asking. If the pane stays waiting but its transcript gains a reply, triage replaces the older card with the new one.

When a pane disappears, its ask and plan cards close as `pane exited`, because they need the live pane. Its information cards close with them only when the pane vanished alone, as when you close its window by hand. Several panes vanishing in one poll, or a dead tmux server, leave information cards queued. Unread reports survive a tmux server death this way. When omp is gone from a pane, whether its window closed, omp crashed or quit, or tmux died, the daemon reads the session once and queues at most one card. A session dispatched from triage, through a brief or an approved plan that carries the status contract, gets a warning card titled `Unreported: <first line of the last reply>` when no final report followed its latest brief. An aborted turn the user interrupted raises no `Unreported:` card. Otherwise a final reply the daemon had not yet carded gets its normal card, an error gets `Stopped with error:`, and a run cut off mid-turn gets a warning titled `Ended mid-turn: <first line of the last message>`. Both warning cards offer Restore run, which opens a new window where `omp --restore` picks the session back up with its todos and context, as its transcript reference names it. Warning cards carry the last reply as body and the transcript as reference. A session the user ran directly that ended cleanly, and a pane he closed from one of its cards, add nothing.

A triage-launched omp pane that comes up at `Error: No model selected.` and writes no transcript is relaunched once. Triage opens a new window in the same session and cwd, runs the argv the pane started with, then kills the dead pane. Omp starts without a model when it cannot read `~/.omp/agent/agent.db` within its busy timeout, which host load causes. A second no-model start of the same command raises an error card titled `Launch failed twice: No model selected` and leaves that pane alone.

A triage-launched omp that exits by itself before it writes a session, with a `SQLITE_BUSY` crash in its own `~/.omp/logs` entry for that process, is relaunched in the same session and cwd after one, two, four and then eight seconds, each with up to 400 ms of jitter. The log entry is what makes the launch safe to repeat; a startup death with another error keeps the old silent behaviour. A launch that dies again after the fifth attempt raises an error card titled `Launch kept dying at startup: database is locked` carrying the command, so it can be started by hand once the host is idle. A bare `omp`, which is the operator's own launch, is never watched this way.

For replies from sessions triage launched, the card shows the title of the card whose answer last launched or briefed the pane, and Task shows that answer and its note, as in `Investigate first · Note: so what should i do?`. Follow-up reports keep the first card's title, and briefs built from them carry both.

The card for a session report shows the outcome in color above its title. A `complete` report reads "Complete" in green, `agent-done` reads "Step left open" in amber unless it closed with `Recommended action: none`, which reads "Complete" in green, `incomplete` reads "Incomplete" in red, and an `Unreported:` card reads "No final report" in amber. A dsh turn that ended while plan mode was still on becomes a plan card instead, because it wrote a plan and stopped for approval; a dsh host too old to decide that plan reports it as a card whose head reads "Plan ready" in the ordinary text color. The title then leaves out its `Done:`, `Agent done:`, `Incomplete:`, `Unreported:` or `Plan ready:` prefix. History rows keep the full title. A launched session's reply card without an `ENVOY_STATUS:` first line keeps `Acknowledge & close` as its primary action and adds `Request final report`. The request types the required status line and report into the waiting pane.

Every finding option opens the Dispatch sheet with its implied mode. The sheet offers Model, Thinking and, for Plan into, an Execution model and Execution effort. Confirm to launch with those choices. The Dispatch and New session sheets also take one image, by the Attach image button or a paste: PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF up to 10 MB. The daemon saves it under `uploads/` beside the briefs and hands it to the engine the way that engine takes one: omp gets an `@` reference in the goal objective, the plan message or the typed brief, and dsh gets an encoded attachment on its launch or prompt. A finding option answered from Dispatch carries the image into its brief too.

Every card carries a context thread one click away: `t`, the Context control in the card dock, or Context on a history row. The thread shows the card's whole story — the dispatching finding, the launches, your steering notes, every report the session produced including superseded ones, and the producer's related cards — oldest first, with the card you opened marked as the anchor.

### Goal mode

Every new omp window triage opens for a brief through a dispatch, a finding answer or the compose sheet starts in goal mode. The objective is the brief's first line and its file path, with an instruction to read the whole brief, so the launch command stays short whatever the brief's size; tmux refuses a command longer than about 16 KiB. Finding answers can choose Investigate, Act, Implement, Plan or Plan into; Plan, Plan into and Implement start those modes instead. A plan dispatch also starts in plan mode. omp records the objective as the session's first user message, and the exit audit recognizes a triage dispatch by the brief path in it. A brief sent to a waiting source pane stays typed text and keeps that pane's modes, except Plan toggles its plan mode.


### Plan mode

When a plan brief opens a new window, a unique per-run config overlay beside the brief starts omp in read-only plan mode. If the card's source pane is already waiting, triage checks its Plan status and sends `M-P` if needed. omp draws the status row only after it resolves the plan file and plan model, so triage polls the row for up to 2 seconds before it sends the brief. A toggle that never shows returns 422.

The tracker waits for a transcript `write` to `xd://propose` and the fullscreen Plan Review overlay. Once both appear, it queues an answerable `plan` card with the redacted plan as its body. The Execution model picker lists omp's `cycleOrder` roles, and Default keeps the role shown in the overlay. Choosing a role moves the slider with Left or Right, waits for the redraw, and checks the selected role before sending Enter. An unavailable role or clipped slider leaves the card queued. The execution model takes the role's own thinking level, because omp's Plan Review slider lists roles only. Refine requires a note; triage moves the `❯` onto `Refine plan` with Up or Down, checks the marked row before Enter, waits for the overlay to close with plan mode still on, then sends the note as steering text. Triage never presses Esc, because that cancels the review and leaves the highlighted row able to approve the plan instead. A new proposal supersedes the old card, and closing the overlay completes it. Every plan brief ends with a status contract because Approve can start a fresh session that sees only the plan. That session's final report then earns its Done card.
Plan and Plan into launches pass the brief's first non-empty line before the `@file` argument. The line is capped at 120 characters, keeping the task visible in OMP's session title without putting the full brief in the command.

### Plan into

Plan into works like envoy's OMP Plan into. A Plan into launch opens a new window running `omp --plan-yolo` with the brief as its prompt. The planner explores read-only and proposes a plan, omp approves that first proposal on its own, and the same session switches to the execution model to carry the plan out. No Plan Review overlay opens and no plan card reaches the inbox, so nobody reviews the plan before it runs; choose Plan when a plan needs a look first. The planner is the model the sheet or the dispatch names, else the daemon's `--model`, else omp's own default. The execution model goes to `--plan-yolo-into`, and when none is chosen omp uses its `smol` role. It keeps the planner's thinking level unless its selector ends in `:<level>`, such as `provider/model:low`, or the launch names an Execution effort. Triage appends an Execution effort to the chosen selector as `:<level>`, or uses `@smol:<level>` when the selector stays at Default. A Plan into dispatch opens a new window even when the card's source pane is waiting, because only launch flags turn plan-yolo on. Its brief asks the planner to end the plan with the status contract, and the execution model's final report earns the normal Done card. The planner and execution fields also offer the configured omp roles as `@role` selectors, so a launch can plan on `@sol` and execute on `@smol`.

### Implement

Implement carries out a plan that is already settled. A Plan dispatch ends in a design, and a second Plan only plans that design again. Implement writes the brief around the plan the card holds and says it is approved: carry it out, keep the steps it lists, ship them through the project's normal pipeline, and do not propose another plan or ask for approval again. On dsh the prompt reads `Implement the plan in the brief at <file>: carry out every step and report the result.`, so the harness prompts the next step as implement instead of asking the session to read the brief a second time. A dsh session still in plan mode is answered through the plan decision instead of prompted, because a prompt in plan mode is planned again: that leaves plan mode, moves the permission from read-only to danger-full-access, and sends the approved plan on. A new session starts in goal mode like Act, and an omp window starts in goal mode with the same instruction in its objective. Implement takes the Model and Thinking controls and needs no Execution model, because the design it carries out is already written.

### dsh sessions

Triage can also launch sessions in dsh, DeepSeek's coding harness. `--engine dsh` makes dsh the daemon's default. The Engine picker in the New session and Dispatch sheets chooses per launch. A dsh session runs in the dsh web host that `dsh-web.service` keeps running. Triage opens no tmux window for it. The daemon reaches the host through its loopback bridge, whose address and secret it reads from `~/.dsh/run/host.json` or from the file that `DSH_HOST_FILE` names.

A dsh session's model and thinking level come from dsh's own catalog. `GET /v1/models?engine=dsh` lists every model the host offers, with the default it gives a session that names none; the pickers offer exactly those selectors, and each model's declared reasoning efforts, so a level dsh would refuse is never offered. The daemon's `--model` applies only while the host still offers it, and otherwise the catalog default wins, which keeps a model that left the settings from reaching a launch. A launch with no choice sends no model, and the bridge selects the catalog default explicitly because every selection becomes dsh's saved default. Nothing in triage names a dsh provider.

Every new dsh session joins the workspace that `--dsh-workspace` names, `triage` by default, so the dsh web UI shows triage's sessions in one group instead of leaving each under Ungrouped. dsh groups a session by the directory it was created in, so a workspace session starts in that workspace's directory rather than the project's. Its brief therefore names the project directory and tells the session to call the `working_directory` tool with `cd` set to it before starting, and a dispatch brief carries the same directory under `## Working directory`. `--dsh-workspace ""` leaves each session in its own directory. A host older than workspaces refuses the launch, and triage then starts the session in the project directory, ungrouped, instead of failing.

dsh names a session from its first user message. A plan or plan-into launch records the brief prompt as one. Triage opens that prompt with the card's task, which gives dsh's title model the work itself instead of a brief path. Until the model answers, dsh's three-word fallback stands. A goal launch only creates a goal. Its rounds are not user messages. dsh has nothing to name from there. Triage sends the title itself for a goal launch, cut between words at 60 characters. That cut keeps every title inside the 80 bytes dsh keeps.

The effort reaches dsh unchanged, because each model declares its own ids: `deepseek-v4.1-flash` accepts `low`, `high` and `max`, and a model with no reasoning control takes no effort. The active host catalog also includes three `command-code` routes with no effort. `beam-free` accepts `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh` and `max`. The host's own list appears when a model is no longer offered or the chosen model rejects its level.

A launch answers the inbox before the bridge does. Start waits two seconds, then answers `202` and leaves the launch running in the daemon for up to two minutes, so a host that is busy starting its first turn never holds the sheet or the phone. A launch that fails after that answer queues a `Could not start the session` card with the bridge's error and the prompt. A slow host is not a failed launch: dsh creates the session before its first command, so a launch that times out adopts the session it started. That is the one session listed in the launch's workspace directory after the launch began, plus the title when the launch sent one. Triage adopts a single matching session. Two matches leave the card on the timeout error instead. The card then points only at a session its launch started. A launch the bridge refuses names the session it created in that answer, and triage archives it before returning the error, so the retry leaves no duplicate behind it.

The tracker polls the bridge every 3 seconds and turns dsh questions, plan reviews and finished turns into the same ask, plan and report cards that omp sessions get. dsh answers `session/list` by projecting every session, which takes a second on a quiet host and ten on a busy one, so only `pending` decides whether the bridge is down: a failed or slow session list skips that poll and retries three seconds later, while a failed `pending` waits thirty seconds and prints the error once. A launch reads the session list only while adopting a session it may have started. A card's source is `dsh://session/<id>` with the label `dsh <session title>`. Peek on a dsh card shows the session's recent messages and tool calls. Send queues a prompt in an idle session, and a launch or prompt from Dispatch can carry an image, which the bridge hands to dsh as an encoded attachment, while Open brings up the dsh web UI on the desktop in place of Jump. Answering in the dsh web UI resolves the card as `answered in dsh`. A host restart resolves the open ask and plan cards as `dsh host restarted`, because dsh does not deliver a waiting question again. Answer multi-select questions in the dsh web UI.
A session whose `goalPhase` is `complete` reports its newest finished turn once the session cursor reaches that turn's end, even when the bridge still marks it `running`. Later events belong to the closing turn and hold the report until that turn ends.

A finished turn of a session still in plan mode earns a plan card instead of a done report, because that turn wrote a plan and no work ran. The card is titled `Plan ready: <plan heading>`, carries the plan as its body, and offers Approve and Refine like a plan review: Approve leaves plan mode with the Execution model and Execution effort, then starts the plan, while Refine sends the note back for more planning. Plan mode comes from the session's `planActive` field. Its plan card answers through the host's `POST /v1/plan`, which a host advertises as `planDecisions` on `GET /v1/sessions`; a host older than that route keeps the plan as a report card whose head reads "Plan ready", because it cannot leave plan mode for you. The turn after a plan card reports normally and supersedes it, and a session the host no longer lists closes its plan card, because nothing is left to approve. A turn that ends without a reply of its own earns no report card, because there is nothing to report. dsh ends a turn that way after an archive, so carding it would repeat the reply the user just closed.

Some dsh sessions are not triage's. An automated producer such as an autorules rule runs there and takes its result from the reply, so its sessions earn no card and would only crowd the inbox. `--dsh-ignore-workspace` names those workspaces, `autorules` by default, and the tracker skips their sessions before it reads an event. dsh creates a workspace session in the workspace's own directory, so the comparison is the session's directory against `<--dsh-workspaces>/<name>`, which defaults to `~/.dsh/workspaces` and must match the dsh-host `--workspaces`. The flag is repeatable, an empty value tracks every workspace, and the daemon refuses an ignore name that equals its own `--dsh-workspace`. Ignoring a workspace does not archive anything: the producer archives its own finished sessions, and `dsh-ask` already does.

Dispatch on a dsh card preselects the dsh engine and sends the brief to the card's session. An Investigate or Act brief is queued there even while the session runs or waits for an answer, because dsh queues a prompt for the next turn, so the work continues in the session the card came from. Plan and Plan into relaunch an idle session in that mode, since a plan changes its permission and plan mode between turns. A busy session for those two modes, a session the host no longer lists, and a prompt the bridge refuses start a new session instead. A dispatch that asks for `new_window` always starts a session of its own. Close archives the session, stops its activity, and resolves its cards. dsh finishes an archive even after its caller gives up, so Close waits 2 seconds for the answer, then resolves the card while dsh finishes; a refusal inside that wait keeps the card queued. Request final report prompts an idle session for its report. Implement on a dsh session still in plan mode answers its plan instead, which leaves plan mode and starts the work. With Plan into, dsh-host approves the first plan itself and switches the session to the execution model, the way omp's `--plan-yolo` does.

A dsh launch names its Execution effort only beside the Execution model it changes, because dsh changes a reasoning effort along a model: a dsh sheet lists no effort level until an execution model is chosen, and its Default row then reads `needs an execution model`. Once a model is chosen, the picker lists the levels that model declares and follows it when the choice changes, and a level chosen for one model is dropped when the execution model returns to Default. dsh-host keeps the effort and applies it to the execution model when the plan is approved; an empty effort keeps the planner's effort. Approving a dsh plan card names both too: the Execution model and the Execution effort. An omp launch needs no model for an effort, which becomes the level of its default smol role.

## Event input

Send CloudEvents 1.0 JSON to `POST /v1/ingest`. The envelope requires `specversion`, `id`, `source`, `type`, and `data`. `subject` and `time` are optional. `priority` is an optional CloudEvents extension from 1 (high) to 5 (low).

For `triage.finding`, `data` may contain `title`, `star` (an object with `situation`, `task`, `action`, and `result`), `options`, `questions`, `refs`, `cwd`, `org_id`, and `label`. Each option has a `label` and may have `description`, `direction`, and `recommended`. A finding question has `label`, `question`, `options`, and optional `allow_other` (default `true`); its answers are sent together in `answers[]`. A finding question may also carry its own `star`; triage shows that text on its step instead of the card's shared `star`, with `label` as the heading. The review step shows the card's shared `star` again. Triage presents each finding question's recommendation, then dispatches accepted Do or Other answers in Act, Implement, Plan or Plan into mode. Skip and Keep do not launch a session. Caller review and supplier prune group a fire's findings on one card. Autorules audits, Etsy ads audits and Upwork analysis post one plain finding card per finding. A finding with the same `source` and exact `title` as a queued finding is stored and immediately marked `done` with reason `duplicate of <queued card id>`. It stays in history and the context thread, but does not queue. Once the original finding is answered or skipped, another matching finding queues normally. For `triage.info`, `data` may contain `title`, `body`, `refs`, and `level`, and an emitter sends it only for a completed job. Other event types are stored without a card.

| POST | `/v1/cards/{id}/later` | Defer a card with a `minutes` value. |
| POST | `/v1/cards/{id}/dispatch` | Hand a brief built from a card to an omp or dsh session. Optional `mode` is `investigate` (default, read-only), `act`, `implement`, `plan` or `plan-into`; unknown mode returns 400. Implement carries out the plan the card holds: an idle dsh session still in plan mode is answered through `/v1/plan`, any other idle session takes the implement prompt, and a new session starts in goal mode. Optional `note` becomes the instruction in the brief. Optional `model` (an omp selector or `@role`) and `thinking` (an `omp --thinking` level) override daemon launch choices for every mode. Optional `engine`, `omp` or `dsh`, picks the harness. A dsh `model` is any selector dsh's catalog offers. `executor` names the plan-into execution model (selector or `@role`) and `executor_thinking` its effort; both require `plan-into`. dsh requires `executor` when `executor_thinking` is set. For omp, an omitted `executor` applies the effort to its default `smol` role. An omp execution effort is appended to the selector as `:<level>`, and a selector that already names one keeps it. Invalid selectors or thinking levels return 400. The brief goes to a waiting source pane or starts in a new window with the selected launch choices; `new_window: true` always starts a window of its own, which the report card's next-step buttons ask for so a finished session is left alone. A dsh brief goes to the card's session: a goal brief is queued there, and a plan or plan-into brief relaunches it while it is idle. A session the host no longer lists, a busy session for a plan, and a prompt the bridge refuses start a new session. Optional `image` is `{media_type, data, name?}`, base64 of a PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF up to 10 MB; an invalid image returns 400. |

| POST | `/v1/cards/{id}/restore` | Open a new tmux window running `omp --restore` with the session id named by the card's transcript, so a crashed or killed run continues with its todos and context. Failures return 422 and leave the card queued. |
| POST | `/v1/cards/{id}/reload` | Put a fresh copy of a handled card back in the queue. Returns `{ok, card}` with the copy's id. 404 when the card is unknown; 409 when the card is queued or deferred, or when a copy of it is already queued. |
| POST | `/v1/sessions` | Launch an omp session from a free `prompt` in `cwd`, default the home directory, as a new window in tmux `session`, default the daemon's `--session` or `triage`. A launch the engine has not finished after two seconds answers `202 { "ok": true, "starting": true }` instead, and keeps running in the daemon: the inbox never waits on a slow host, and a launch that fails after that answer queues a `Could not start the session` card carrying the error and the prompt. |
| GET | `/v1/tmux-sessions` | Return `{sessions, default, directories}`, the current tmux sessions the New session sheet offers, the default one, and the distinct working directories of tmux panes, those most panes share first. |
| GET | `/v1/models?engine=NAME` | Return `{engine, models, execution_models, default, thinking, default_thinking}` for the engine the query names, `omp` or `dsh`, else for the daemon's `--engine`. Each entry has `{selector, name}`. For omp, `models` contains catalog-backed `@role` entries whose configured models are in the unfiltered `omp models --json` catalog, followed by the catalog rows that `enabledModels` allows. Roles on models outside the catalog, such as `@web` on `web/searxng`, are left out. `execution_models` lists configured catalog-backed roles from omp's Plan Review slider order. A failed model or cycle-order query is retried at the next 30-second check even when omp's config is unchanged. `default` and `default_thinking` come from daemon launch flags, or `null` when omp defaults apply. Both appear only when omp is the daemon's engine. For dsh, `models` and `execution_models` hold the live dsh catalog, each entry `{selector, name, efforts}`, where `efforts` lists the reasoning levels that model accepts. `thinking` holds dsh's fallback levels, and `default` is the catalog default, or the daemon's `--model` while the host still offers it. A host that cannot be read leaves the list empty and `default` null, so no picker offers a model dsh would refuse. New session offers Engine, Model, Thinking, tmux session and directory pickers, while Dispatch shares Engine, Model and Thinking. A model field offers the catalog of the engine its Engine field names. Choosing another engine empties that list until its catalog arrives, and the newest read is the one that fills it: a catalog that answers after the engine moved on never lands in the field, which a slow dsh bridge makes possible. |
| GET | `/v1/history?limit=50` | Return handled cards, newest first, as `{card, swipe}` rows. Each card carries only `id`, `kind`, `title`, `source` and its option labels. |
| GET | `/v1/dictation` | Return `{dictionary, phrases}`, the saved dictation settings, both empty strings until the first save. |
| POST | `/v1/dictation` | Store `{dictionary, phrases}` exactly as typed and return `{ok:true}`. The inbox checks each dictionary line before it saves. |
| GET | `/v1/cards/{id}/context` | Return one card's context thread as `{anchor, items, truncated}`. Items are `{card, swipes}` rows, oldest first, carrying full cards with bodies and every swipe. The thread joins the card, its transcript siblings, the cards of the panes it lived in within 48 h of each mention, the cards that launched those panes, and for an ingested card the newest 50 cards of its source, capped at the newest 100. 404 when the card is unknown. |
| GET | `/v1/panes/{pane}/peek` | Return the pane's redacted visible screen with its `width` and `height` in cells and its asking, waiting, working, or other state. |
| POST | `/v1/panes/{pane}/send` | Send text to a waiting omp pane when its editor is empty and text passes safety checks. |
| POST | `/v1/panes/{pane}/jump` | Jump tmux to a pane. |
| POST | `/v1/dsh/open` | Open the dsh web UI on the daemon's desktop. 409 with the reason when dsh-host is unreachable or refuses. |
| GET | `/v1/dsh/{id}/peek` | Return the dsh session's redacted recent messages and tool calls as `{text, state, width, height}`, where `state` is asking, working or waiting. 404 when dsh-host does not know the session. |
| POST | `/v1/dsh/{id}/send` | Queue `text` as a prompt in an idle dsh session. 409 when the session is busy or the text starts with a blocked command prefix. |

## CLI

- `triage serve [--listen 127.0.0.1:7340] [--db PATH] [--session NAME] [--engine omp|dsh] [--model SELECTOR] [--thinking LEVEL] [--dsh-workspace NAME] [--dsh-workspaces DIR] [--dsh-ignore-workspace NAME]... [--allow-host HOST]...` starts the server and pane tracker.
- `triage emit [FILE|-]` posts a CloudEvent from a JSON file or standard input.
- `triage finding --source URI --title T [--situation TEXT] [--task TEXT] [--action TEXT] [--result TEXT] [--option LABEL]... [--ref PATH]... [--org-id ID] [--cwd DIR] [--priority N] [--id ID]` posts a finding event.
- `deploy/finding-example.sh` shows a scheduled job posting a finding.

The client commands use `TRIAGE_URL` when set. Otherwise they connect to `http://127.0.0.1:7340`.

## Build and install

Build with `cargo build --release`, then copy `target/release/triage` to `~/.local/bin/triage`. Install `deploy/triage.service` as `~/.config/systemd/user/triage.service`, then run `systemctl --user enable --now triage.service`.

## Requirements

Triage needs Linux, tmux, and `omp` on `PATH`. The dsh engine also requires the dsh web host, which `dsh-web.service` runs. Building requires Rust 1.89 or newer, because the locked `uuid 1.28.0` declares `rust-version = "1.89"`. The test suite also needs Node.js, `curl`, `jq`, and `ss`.

## Configuration

Settings are `triage serve` flags; there is no configuration file. Put them on the `ExecStart` line of your copy of `deploy/triage.service`.

| Flag | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--listen` | `127.0.0.1:7340` | Address the daemon binds. |
| `--db` | `$XDG_DATA_HOME/triage/triage.db` or `~/.local/share/triage/triage.db` | SQLite database; briefs go in `briefs/` beside it. |
| `--session` | `triage` | tmux session new windows open in. The session must exist. |
| `--engine` | `omp` | Harness for launched sessions, `omp` or `dsh`. A request can override it in the New session or Dispatch sheet, or on a finding answer. |
| `--model` | omp's own model | Default model for new windows. A request can override it in the New session or Dispatch sheet, or on a finding answer. It takes an omp selector or `@role`; omp resolves a role when each window starts. A brief sent to a waiting source pane keeps that pane's model, except Plan mode can switch it to the pane's own `plan` role. With `--engine dsh` it takes any selector from dsh's catalog; empty takes the host's default. |
| `--thinking` | omp's own level | Thinking level for launched sessions; accepts `auto`, `off`, `minimal`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`, `max`. |
| `--dsh-workspace` | `triage` | dsh workspace every new dsh session joins, so the dsh web UI groups triage's sessions. It is one lowercase name; dsh-host keeps the workspace in `~/.dsh/workspaces/<name>`. Empty starts each session in its project directory, ungrouped. |
| `--dsh-workspaces` | `~/.dsh/workspaces` | Directory dsh-host keeps one directory per workspace in, the dsh-host `--workspaces`. The ignored-workspace comparison uses it. |
| `--dsh-ignore-workspace` | `autorules` | Repeatable. dsh workspace whose sessions never become cards, because an automated producer runs there. An empty value tracks every workspace. A name equal to `--dsh-workspace` stops the daemon at startup. |
| `--allow-host` | none | Repeatable. Host names accepted besides `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` and `::1`. |

An invalid `--model` or `--thinking` stops the daemon at startup, for example `ExecStart=%h/.local/bin/triage serve --session work --model provider/model-id --thinking high --allow-host my-machine.my-tailnet.ts.net`. Client commands read `TRIAGE_URL`.

## Web inbox

The web UI is served at `/`. Keyboard controls are digits to choose an option, and the digit after the last option opens Other; a finding option opens Dispatch with its implied mode. `a` or Enter opens Dispatch preselected to Plan on report cards that offer Plan next step, and `d` opens it preselected to Act for Do next step. Confirm in the sheet to launch. `o` opens Other text (submitting it empty keeps the field open and says to type an answer), `n` focuses the note, `i` opens Dispatch with Investigate, `A` preselects Act, `p` preselects Plan, `P` preselects Plan into, and `I` preselects Implement. Dispatch has Mode, Engine, Model and Thinking controls, plus Execution model and Execution effort for Plan into. Plan approval offers the same two execution fields on a dsh plan card. `x` acknowledges and closes a card's tmux pane, `r` requests the final report from a reply card that missed its status line, `R` restores the run of an `Unreported:` or `Ended mid-turn:` card in a new window, `s` skips, `l` defers, `v` peeks at the source pane, `t` opens context, `J` jumps to the pane, `c` opens Compose for a new session with Goal, Plan or Plan into mode, `h` opens history, `j` and `k` move through cards, and `?` opens help. On info cards with a pane, Acknowledge & close remains primary. Cards without a recommended step retain their current acknowledge actions.

The top bar and the card share one centered column. The card head shows the card's place in the deck, such as `1 of 7`, beside a rail with one bar per waiting card in deck order. Higher-priority cards get taller bars, and the shown card's bar is blue. A higher-priority arrival moves the view to the deck's top card. An open Later menu or typed answer text on the shown card keeps it in place. Under the title, a row of facts gives the source, directory, pane, kind, priority and waiting time. From 1100px wide, the evidence (Situation, Plan, Task and Result) fills the left column and the Action options fill the right, so the answer stays beside what it answers. When the window is also at least 760px tall, the Action column stays in view while the evidence scrolls. Narrower screens stack the evidence above the Action block, which then sits next to the dock. Plan cards show their plan under Plan, and info cards show their final reply only under Result. Card text renders as Markdown. The evidence blocks and the final reply show headings, lists, tables, quotes, code blocks, emphasis and links, and titles and option text show code and emphasis without links. Raw HTML shows as literal text, only http, https and mailto links become links, each opens in a new tab, and a remote image in the text shows as a link instead of loading. A body that parses as JSON shows as one code block. The parser is marked, vendored as `ui/marked.js` with its MIT license in `ui/marked.LICENSE.md`, and the inbox builds the elements from its tokens, so card text never passes through `innerHTML`. When an ask's task repeats its title, the card has no separate Task block. Dock buttons show their keys, as do header buttons on screens wider than 800px, and touch screens hide every key hint. On phones the facts drop their labels and run together under the source name, and the four counts fold into a single total that opens them. The text uses Geist and Geist Mono, and the daemon serves both fonts from `ui/fonts/`.

The sliders button in the top bar opens Settings, which holds the Dictation form. A microphone appears beside the card's Note, the Other answer, the text for the pane in Peek, the New session prompt and the Dispatch instruction whenever the browser offers speech recognition on a secure page, served over HTTPS or from localhost. A browser that can recognize speech on the device is told to do so: the inbox reads `SpeechRecognition` or `webkitSpeechRecognition`, takes the on-device path only when `available()` and `processLocally` exist, and sets `processLocally` on every recognition. Chrome 139 or later and Firefox 160 or later offer that on a desktop. Safari offers only `webkitSpeechRecognition`, which sends the audio to Apple's speech service, so the microphones work on iPhone, iPad and Safari through that service, and the Settings sheet says the words leave the device. iOS 14.5 or later also needs Dictation and Siri enabled in the device's Settings; a page cannot turn that on, and Safari reports no speech without it. Audio never reaches the daemon. The first press on a site sets up the browser's speech model for that site, which can take a few minutes. After that, a press starts listening and a second press stops it. Every recognition is continuous, so Safari 17 or later, Chrome and Firefox keep listening across short pauses. Safari before 17 still ends a run at the first pause; the run then finishes with the words it heard, and the next press starts it again. The page keeps one recogniser and reuses it for every run, because iPhone can ask for the microphone again for a new one. A recogniser that never reports starting is dropped, and a start or stop the browser never answers ends within seconds with a message instead of a microphone that answers no press. A hidden page drops the run, as WebKit refuses speech recognition while the page is not visible. The words go in at the cursor each time the browser finishes recognizing a stretch of speech, read by index from the array-like result list, which has no iterator. Each Dictionary line reads `heard = written`. The inbox matches the heard form as whole words in any letter case and puts the written form in its place. Each line of Phrases you often say becomes a button under the New session prompt and the Dispatch instruction. Pressing it puts the phrase in at the cursor. The daemon keeps both lists for all devices. `GET /v1/dictation` reads them and `POST /v1/dictation` saves them.


When a card action advances the inbox, the next card enters from the right. Answering a card below the top returns to the top-ranked card from the left.

A card answer or dispatch never opens a window in the session you are working in. A brief goes to its waiting source pane when available; otherwise a new window opens in the daemon's `--session`, default `triage`. The New session sheet can choose another session. Dispatch and finding answers use the daemon's session and the card's cwd. Every new window uses the daemon's `--model` and `--thinking` unless the request overrides them. Setting `--model @default` follows omp's current default; when unset, omp applies its defaults. Windows stay unnamed so tmux keeps `automatic-rename` and the status bar follows omp's pane title. New sessions leave the inbox open, and their asks and final reports arrive as cards. The New session sheet's Start hands the launch to the daemon and closes at once. The sheet never waits on a slow host: the daemon answers within two seconds, and a launch it has not finished reports `Starting a dsh session.` and finishes behind the inbox. A toast names the pane when the session starts that fast. If the launch fails before the answer, the sheet reopens with its prompt back; if it fails after, a `Could not start the session` card carries the error and the prompt.

When several devices use the inbox, every change appears on each client within a second. If another client handles the shown card, the inbox announces `Handled elsewhere` and moves on. The daemon refuses a second answer with 409. An action moves the inbox to the top-ranked waiting card at once while the daemon types the answer in the background. Card actions on one pane run one at a time. If the action then fails, such as a tmux launch error, the daemon returns 422 with the reason. The inbox shows the error in a toast while the card returns to the deck with its draft. Toasts report an error or a session launch's progress: `Starting…` stays up until the daemon answers, then reads `Started · <pane>`, or `Starting a dsh session.` when the daemon accepted the launch and has not finished it. Every other successful answer, skip, copy, jump or reload shows none. The screen-reader announcer still reads each result.
An open inbox follows daemon upgrades. Each queue response names the inbox build the daemon serves in `shell`. A tab that loaded another build reloads itself. A phone app left open, or a tab opened from a Home Screen icon, then stops running old code against new cards. The reload waits while a dialog is open, an action is in flight, or typed text sits in a card's Note, Other or Dispatch field or in the New session prompt. When the worker itself changes, it pings every open tab and reloads the ones that do not answer, so a tab older than the shell check still picks up the current inbox.

Long titles clamp to two lines on every card, with a Show more toggle in the header that reveals the rest. Error toasts and history rows clamp long titles the same way. This keeps a card's long title from pushing the actions out of reach or covering the screen. Error toasts sit bottom-center on every viewport.

`tests/ui-checks.js` holds the browser regression checks. Open the inbox, paste the file into the DevTools console or load it with the Chrome MCP `evaluate_script` tool, then call `checkTriage('<scenario>')` and read the result or assertion message. Scenarios are `peek`, `dimensions`, `portrait` and `landscape`, which need the pane peek open and loaded first, plus `offline`, `recovery`, `finding-other`, `compose-stays`, `picker`, `models`, `dispatch`, `history-retry`, `long-title`, `mobile-card`, `pwa`, `plan-into`, `plan-approval`, `header`, `dsh-card` and `dictation`, which run from the inbox view. `long-title` needs the shown card to carry a title longer than two lines, `mobile-card` needs a card with options in a viewport at most 639px wide, and `plan-into` and `plan-approval` need a plan card in the queue. `dsh-card` requires a dsh card in the queue and checks that it offers Open and Peek in place of Jump. `finding-other` needs a finding card shown. On a finding question it expects a click on Other to open its field, an empty submit to say so and stay put, and typed text to move on without posting an answer. On a plain finding it expects no Other. `picker` checks the full-screen phone layout at most 639px wide and the dropdown layout above that. `models` holds one engine's catalog read open while the Engine field moves to the other engine, then releases it, and expects each model field to keep the catalog of the engine it names. `models` holds one engine's catalog read open while the Engine field moves to the other engine, then releases it, and expects each model field to keep the catalog of the engine it names. `header` checks that the header fits and nothing in it is clipped. `dictation` needs a saved phrase and swaps in fake speech engines, which lets it run in any browser. It opens Settings, presses a phrase button, then dictates into the New session prompt and checks that the recognizer started with `processLocally` set. It then swaps in Safari's engine, which has neither `available()` nor `processLocally`, and checks that the microphones stay visible, that the recognizer starts without those, that it is continuous, and that the next press reuses that one recognizer instead of asking for the microphone again.


For remote access, publish the server on a tailnet with `tailscale serve --bg --https=7340 http://127.0.0.1:7340`. That gives `https://<machine>.<tailnet>.ts.net:7340/` with a Tailscale certificate and tailnet-only reach. Start the daemon with `--allow-host <machine>.<tailnet>.ts.net` so it accepts that host name. The published port must match the `--listen` port or be the default HTTPS port. The server has no authentication of its own, so never expose it beyond the tailnet. `tailscale serve --https=7340 off` removes it.

On iPhone and iPad, connect the Tailscale app before opening the inbox. A `ts.net` name resolves only through Tailscale's DNS. Without the tunnel there is nothing to reach. iOS suspends the app in the background. Turning on Connect on Demand in the Tailscale app keeps it running. Some Safari settings reroute DNS around Tailscale. These include iCloud Private Relay, Limit IP Address Tracking, or an encrypted-DNS profile. With any of them on, the name fails to resolve even while the tunnel is up. Try the Home Screen icon first, since it is not a Safari tab and avoids those Safari-only settings.

## Scope and limits

The default address is `http://127.0.0.1:7340`. Triage understands two harnesses, omp in tmux panes and dsh through the dsh web host. Omp's native Plan Review overlay is the first signature in a generic screen-signature to card to keys relay. Claude Code's `--permission-mode plan` and other harnesses can use the same path as future signature rows, without new plumbing. Multi-select and multi-checkbox asks stay read-only in the inbox and must be answered in the pane. HTML asks support radios, single selects, lone checkboxes, and text fields. Multi-question asks are answered step by step. A `triage.finding` with native `questions` gets one answer per finding. A question with its own `star` shows only that finding on its step, while the review step returns to the card's shared content. Caller review and supplier prune keep one card per fire under a per-fire event ID. Autorules audits, Etsy ads audits and Upwork analysis post one plain finding card per finding. Question-level `allow_other` controls free-text answers. Finding answers dispatch in Act, Plan, or Plan into mode. Skip and Keep resolve without launching. A restored pane that gets the same pane id its reply had before keeps the old card id, which still blocks a re-queue. A report from a session omp resumed under a new pane id after a tmux restart, or one the exit audit queues after its pane is gone, has no Situation or Task.
A pane that vanishes while the daemon is down is never audited, because the tracker's seen-set lives in memory.

## Known Issues

Two performance limits are known. Each poll tick scans and parses every open and deferred card under the database mutex, and the poller runs the full queue query twice per tick. Open behavior limits are the ones under Scope and limits.
