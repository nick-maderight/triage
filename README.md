# Triage

Triage is one decision inbox for scheduled jobs and for omp and dsh sessions. It shows one card at a time in priority order. Ask cards show the STAR situation, task, action, and result with options suited to the situation. Skip and later are built in.

## Agent sessions

Triage polls tmux once a second. It recognizes omp pane states from pane titles, then reads the pane's transcript file to detect pending questions and final reports. It sends selected answers with guarded tmux `send-keys`. Agents need no triage-side code or plugin.

A reply keeps one card when tmux restarts and omp resumes it under a new pane id: a reply already dealt with stays handled, and a reply whose card is still queued from the old pane moves to the new pane. The tracker trusts a pane's omp breadcrumb only when it was written after the pane's pty appeared, or when the pane's omp was started with `--resume` or `--restore` of that session.
The inbox holds cards that need a decision. An omp run that stops earns an information card. omp shows the same waiting title for a finished run, an interrupted one, and one waiting on background jobs. Triage reads the last assistant message's stop reason. A finished reply earns a card, while errors earn a `Stopped with error` card with an error label and interrupts earn none. A run whose last message is a tool call, whose working row shows a background-job wait or automatic retry, or whose reply comes before a delivered background-job result or supervised-process exit has not stopped yet. Its card comes when it does. omp redraws the working row on every spinner tick. Triage reads three captures before queuing a card. omp drops the wait row when a background job finishes, seconds before the result reaches the transcript. A reply last seen with the row stays held until the transcript changes or 30 seconds pass without the row. A reply counts as a final report when its first line is a status line such as `ENVOY_STATUS: complete`, `ENVOY_STATUS: agent-done`, or `ENVOY_STATUS: incomplete`. Bold, code ticks or a heading mark around that line still count, and text after the status word on the same line starts the report. Every triage brief requires one. Every brief tells the session to finish every step its instructions allow, such as the checks, commit, push or deploy, before it reports. It then asks for a caveman-style report of four short lines at most, with the outcome first so it becomes the card title. The report ends in a `Recommended action:` line that names the one change the session was not allowed to make and the user must approve, or reads `Recommended action: none`. Work waiting on a later event and steps only the user can take also read `none`. Briefs define `complete` as work released through the project's normal pipeline with its full test gate passed. Envoys report `agent-done` when they finish their work but a required push, deploy, or full test gate remains open. Each report names the open step. A final report written before a background wait stays the card's report, and the closing reply follows it in the body. A reply without a status line also ends the run, unless omp will start the session's next turn itself. That is the case while the session owns a supervised process (omp `bash` with `name`) that was started without a readiness gate less than two hours ago, or that exited less than a minute ago and whose exit omp has not yet reported. It is also the case for up to 30 seconds after the transcript last changed while the session's goal is active. A server started with a readiness gate never holds a card. A final reply never waits. A reply that ends the run earns the same card showing its first line as the title and the reply as its body. Reports with no status line have no outcome label. A pane with no assistant message yet makes no card. A newer card of the same session replaces an older one still queued. Triage removes `Recommended action: none` and empty labels. It moves recommendations under Action on investigations and execution reports with status `agent-done` or `incomplete`. Complete reports from other launches keep recommendations in the body. Acknowledge stays the primary action. Investigations still send their step to a waiting pane as a plan when its editor is empty and no goal is active. A reply without a status line can show both `Plan recommended step` and `Request final report`. Carried replies follow the same status and launch-mode rule. Ended-session warnings and ingested `triage.info` bodies still move steps under Action. Finding cards show their Action and options. Ask cards show their choices. Plan cards show their proposals. Error cards keep their diagnostic text.
omp drops the background-job wait row when the job finishes, seconds before its result reaches the transcript. Triage holds any reply last seen with that row for up to 30 seconds while the transcript stays unchanged.
Resuming a session in its pane closes its queued and deferred report cards as `resumed in pane` when its title changes from waiting to working or asking. If the pane stays waiting but its transcript gains a reply, triage replaces the older card with the new one.

When a pane disappears, its ask and plan cards close as `pane exited`, because they need the live pane. Its information cards close with them only when the pane vanished alone, as when you close its window by hand. Several panes vanishing in one poll, or a dead tmux server, leave information cards queued. Unread reports survive a tmux server death this way. When omp is gone from a pane, whether its window closed, omp crashed or quit, or tmux died, the daemon reads the session once and queues at most one card. A session dispatched from triage, through a brief or an approved plan that carries the status contract, gets a warning card titled `Unreported: <first line of the last reply>` when no final report followed its latest brief. An aborted turn the user interrupted raises no `Unreported:` card. Otherwise a final reply the daemon had not yet carded gets its normal card, an error gets `Stopped with error:`, and a run cut off mid-turn gets a warning titled `Ended mid-turn: <first line of the last message>`. Both warning cards offer Restore run, which opens a new window where `omp --restore` picks the session back up with its todos and context, as its transcript reference names it. Warning cards carry the last reply as body and the transcript as reference. A session the user ran directly that ended cleanly, and a pane he closed from one of its cards, add nothing.

A triage-launched omp pane that comes up at `Error: No model selected.` and writes no transcript is relaunched once. Triage opens a new window in the same session and cwd, runs the argv the pane started with, then kills the dead pane. Omp starts without a model when it cannot read `~/.omp/agent/agent.db` within its busy timeout, which host load causes. A second no-model start of the same command raises an error card titled `Launch failed twice: No model selected` and leaves that pane alone.

A triage-launched omp that exits by itself before it writes a session, with a `SQLITE_BUSY` crash in its own `~/.omp/logs` entry for that process, is relaunched in the same session and cwd after one, two, four and then eight seconds, each with up to 400 ms of jitter. The log entry is what makes the launch safe to repeat; a startup death with another error keeps the old silent behaviour. A launch that dies again after the fifth attempt raises an error card titled `Launch kept dying at startup: database is locked` carrying the command, so it can be started by hand once the host is idle. A bare `omp`, which is the operator's own launch, is never watched this way.

For replies from sessions triage launched, the card shows the title of the card whose answer last launched or briefed the pane, and Task shows that answer and its note, as in `Investigate first · Note: so what should i do?`. Follow-up reports keep the first card's title, and briefs built from them carry both.

The card for a session report shows the outcome in color above its title. A `complete` report reads "Complete" in green, `agent-done` reads "Step left open" in amber, `incomplete` reads "Incomplete" in red, and an `Unreported:` card reads "No final report" in amber. A dsh turn that ended while plan mode was still on reads "Plan ready" in the ordinary text color, because it wrote a plan and stopped for approval. The title then leaves out its `Done:`, `Agent done:`, `Incomplete:`, `Unreported:` or `Plan ready:` prefix. History rows keep the full title. A report card also shows the images its turn read, up to twelve, as thumbnails under Result: omp's image blocks come from its blob store, and dsh's from its attachment store. A thumbnail opens the full image in a new tab. Only PNG, JPEG, WebP and GIF are served, and images from earlier turns are left out. A launched session's reply card without an `ENVOY_STATUS:` first line keeps `Acknowledge & close` as its primary action and adds `Request final report`. The request types the required status line and report into the waiting pane.

Every finding option opens the Dispatch sheet with its implied mode. The sheet offers Model, Thinking and, for Plan into, an Execution model and Execution effort. Confirm to launch with those choices.

Every card carries a context thread one click away: `t`, the Context control in the card dock, or Context on a history row. The thread shows the card's whole story — the dispatching finding, the launches, your steering notes, every report the session produced including superseded ones, and the producer's related cards — oldest first, with the card you opened marked as the anchor.

### Goal mode

Every new omp window triage opens for a brief through a dispatch, a finding answer or the compose sheet starts in goal mode with the brief text as its objective. Finding answers can choose Investigate, Act, Plan or Plan into; Plan and Plan into start those modes instead. A plan dispatch also starts in plan mode. The objective ends with the brief file's path because omp records it as the session's first user message and the exit audit recognizes a triage dispatch by that path. A brief sent to a waiting source pane stays typed text and keeps that pane's modes, except Plan toggles its plan mode.

### Plan mode

When a plan brief opens a new window, a unique per-run config overlay beside the brief starts omp in read-only plan mode. If the card's source pane is already waiting, triage checks its Plan status and sends `M-P` if needed. omp draws the status row only after it resolves the plan file and plan model, so triage polls the row for up to 2 seconds before it sends the brief. A toggle that never shows returns 422.

The tracker waits for a transcript `write` to `xd://propose` and the fullscreen Plan Review overlay. Once both appear, it queues an answerable `plan` card with the redacted plan as its body. The Execution model picker lists omp's `cycleOrder` roles, and Default keeps the role shown in the overlay. Choosing a role moves the slider with Left or Right, waits for the redraw, and checks the selected role before sending Enter. An unavailable role or clipped slider leaves the card queued. The execution model takes the role's own thinking level, because omp's Plan Review slider lists roles only. Refine requires a note; triage sends Esc to close the overlay without discarding the plan, then sends the note as steering text. A new proposal supersedes the old card, and closing the overlay completes it. Every plan brief ends with a status contract because Approve can start a fresh session that sees only the plan. That session's final report then earns its Done card.

### Plan into

Plan into works like envoy's OMP Plan into. A Plan into launch opens a new window running `omp --plan-yolo` with the brief as its prompt. The planner explores read-only and proposes a plan, omp approves that first proposal on its own, and the same session switches to the execution model to carry the plan out. No Plan Review overlay opens and no plan card reaches the inbox, so nobody reviews the plan before it runs; choose Plan when a plan needs a look first. The planner is the model the sheet or the dispatch names, else the daemon's `--model`, else omp's own default. The execution model goes to `--plan-yolo-into`, and when none is chosen omp uses its `smol` role. It keeps the planner's thinking level unless its selector ends in `:<level>`, such as `provider/model:low`, or the launch names an Execution effort. Triage appends an Execution effort to the chosen selector as `:<level>`, or uses `@smol:<level>` when the selector stays at Default. A Plan into dispatch opens a new window even when the card's source pane is waiting, because only launch flags turn plan-yolo on. Its brief asks the planner to end the plan with the status contract, and the execution model's final report earns the normal Done card. The planner and execution fields also offer the configured omp roles as `@role` selectors, so a launch can plan on `@sol` and execute on `@smol`.

### dsh sessions

Triage can also launch sessions in dsh, DeepSeek's coding harness. `--engine dsh` makes dsh the daemon's default. The Engine picker in the New session and Dispatch sheets chooses per launch. A dsh session runs in the dsh web host that `dsh-web.service` keeps running. Triage opens no tmux window for it. The daemon reaches the host through its loopback bridge, whose address and secret it reads from `~/.dsh/run/host.json` or from the file that `DSH_HOST_FILE` names.

A dsh session's model and thinking level come from dsh's own catalog. `GET /v1/models?engine=dsh` lists every model the host offers, with the default it gives a session that names none; the pickers offer exactly those selectors, and each model's declared reasoning efforts, so a level dsh would refuse is never offered. The daemon's `--model` applies only while the host still offers it, and otherwise the catalog default wins, which keeps a model that left the settings from reaching a launch. A launch with no choice sends no model, and the bridge selects the catalog default explicitly because every selection becomes dsh's saved default. Nothing in triage names a dsh provider.

Every new dsh session joins the workspace that `--dsh-workspace` names, `triage` by default, so the dsh web UI shows triage's sessions in one group instead of leaving each under Ungrouped. dsh groups a session by the directory it was created in, so a workspace session starts in that workspace's directory rather than the project's. Its brief therefore names the project directory and tells the session to call the `working_directory` tool with `cd` set to it before starting, and a dispatch brief carries the same directory under `## Working directory`. `--dsh-workspace ""` leaves each session in its own directory. A host older than workspaces refuses the launch, and triage then starts the session in the project directory, ungrouped, instead of failing.

The effort reaches dsh unchanged, because each model declares its own ids: `deepseek-v4.1-flash` accepts `low`, `high` and `max`, `beam-free` accepts `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh` and `max`, and a model with no reasoning control has no effort at all. A model the host no longer offers, or a level the chosen model does not accept, fails the action with the host's own list in the message.

The tracker polls the bridge every 3 seconds and turns dsh questions, plan reviews and finished turns into the same ask, plan and report cards that omp sessions get. A card's source is `dsh://session/<id>` with the label `dsh <session title>`. Peek on a dsh card shows the session's recent messages and tool calls, and the bridge names a tool result's images in its events, so the card serves them from dsh's attachment store under `$DSH_HOME` or `~/.dsh`. Send queues a prompt in an idle session, while Open brings up the dsh web UI on the desktop in place of Jump. Answering in the dsh web UI resolves the card as `answered in dsh`. A host restart resolves the open ask and plan cards as `dsh host restarted`, because dsh does not deliver a waiting question again. Answer multi-select questions in the dsh web UI.

A finished turn of a session still in plan mode reports `Plan ready: <plan heading>` instead of a done report. The card carries that plan as its body and leaves out the final-report request and the recommended step. Plan mode comes from the session's `planActive` field. After approval, the next turn reports normally and its card supersedes the plan-ready one.

Some dsh sessions are not triage's. An automated producer such as an autorules rule runs there and takes its result from the reply, so its sessions earn no card and would only crowd the inbox. `--dsh-ignore-workspace` names those workspaces, `autorules` by default, and the tracker skips their sessions before it reads an event. dsh creates a workspace session in the workspace's own directory, so the comparison is the session's directory against `<--dsh-workspaces>/<name>`, which defaults to `~/.dsh/workspaces` and must match the dsh-host `--workspaces`. The flag is repeatable, an empty value tracks every workspace, and the daemon refuses an ignore name that equals its own `--dsh-workspace`. Ignoring a workspace does not archive anything: the producer archives its own finished sessions, and `dsh-ask` already does.

Dispatch on a dsh card preselects the dsh engine and sends the brief to the same session when it is idle. Plan into, or a busy session, starts a new session. Close archives the session, stops its activity, and resolves its cards. Request final report prompts an idle session for its report. With Plan into, dsh-host approves the first plan itself and switches the session to the execution model, the way omp's `--plan-yolo` does.

A dsh launch names its Execution effort beside the model. dsh-host keeps it and applies it to the execution model when the plan is approved; empty keeps the planner's effort. Approving a dsh plan card names both too: the Execution model and the Execution effort. The effort picker lists the levels the chosen execution model declares, and follows the model when the choice changes.

## Event input

Send CloudEvents 1.0 JSON to `POST /v1/ingest`. The envelope requires `specversion`, `id`, `source`, `type`, and `data`. `subject` and `time` are optional. `priority` is an optional CloudEvents extension from 1 (high) to 5 (low).

For `triage.finding`, `data` may contain `title`, `star` (an object with `situation`, `task`, `action`, and `result`), `options`, `refs`, `cwd`, `org_id`, and `label`. Each option has a `label` and may have `description`, `direction`, and `recommended`. A finding with the same `source` and exact `title` as a queued finding is stored and immediately marked `done` with reason `duplicate of <queued card id>`. It stays in history and the context thread, but does not queue, notify or push. Once the original finding is answered or skipped, another matching finding queues normally. For `triage.info`, `data` may contain `title`, `body`, `refs`, and `level`, and an emitter sends it only for a completed job. Other event types are stored without a card.

## CLI

- `triage serve [--listen 127.0.0.1:7340] [--db PATH] [--session NAME] [--engine omp|dsh] [--model SELECTOR] [--thinking LEVEL] [--dsh-workspace NAME] [--dsh-workspaces DIR] [--dsh-ignore-workspace NAME]... [--allow-host HOST]... [--no-notify]` starts the server and pane tracker.
- `triage emit [FILE|-]` posts a CloudEvent from a JSON file or standard input.
- `triage finding --source URI --title T [--situation TEXT] [--task TEXT] [--action TEXT] [--result TEXT] [--option LABEL]... [--ref PATH]... [--org-id ID] [--cwd DIR] [--priority N] [--id ID]` posts a finding event.
- `deploy/finding-example.sh` shows a scheduled job posting a finding.

The client commands use `TRIAGE_URL` when set. Otherwise they connect to `http://127.0.0.1:7340`.

## Build and install

Build with `cargo build --release`, then copy `target/release/triage` to `~/.local/bin/triage`. Install `deploy/triage.service` as `~/.config/systemd/user/triage.service`, then run `systemctl --user enable --now triage.service`.

## Requirements

Triage needs Linux, tmux, and `omp` on `PATH`; `notify-send` provides the desktop notifications, and `--no-notify` turns them off. The dsh engine also requires the dsh web host, which `dsh-web.service` runs. Browser push notifications need the inbox on HTTPS or localhost, and on iPhone and iPad they need iOS 16.4 or newer with the inbox added to the Home Screen. Building requires Rust 1.89 or newer, because the locked `uuid 1.28.0` declares `rust-version = "1.89"`. The test suite also needs Node.js, `curl`, `jq`, and `ss`.

## Configuration

Settings are `triage serve` flags; there is no configuration file. Put them on the `ExecStart` line of your copy of `deploy/triage.service`.

| Flag | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--listen` | `127.0.0.1:7340` | Address the daemon binds. |
| `--db` | `$XDG_DATA_HOME/triage/triage.db` or `~/.local/share/triage/triage.db` | SQLite database; briefs go in `briefs/` beside it. |
| `--session` | `triage` | tmux session new windows open in. The session must exist. |
| `--engine` | `omp` | Harness for launched sessions, `omp` or `dsh`. A request can override it in the New session or Dispatch sheet, or on a finding answer. |
| `--model` | omp's own model | Default model for new windows. A request can override it in the New session or Dispatch sheet, or on a finding answer. It takes an omp selector or `@role`; omp resolves a role when each window starts. A brief sent to a waiting source pane keeps that pane's model, except Plan mode can switch it to the pane's own `plan` role. With `--engine dsh` it takes any selector from dsh's catalog; empty takes the host's default. |
| `--thinking` | omp's own level | Thinking level for launched sessions; accepts `auto`, `off`, `minimal`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`, `max`. |
| `--dsh-workspace` | `triage` | dsh workspace every new dsh session joins, so the dsh web UI groups triage's sessions. It is one lowercase name; dsh-host keeps the workspace in `~/.dsh/workspaces/<name>`. Empty starts each session in its project directory, ungrouped. |
| `--dsh-workspaces` | `~/.dsh/workspaces` | Directory dsh-host keeps one directory per workspace in, the dsh-host `--workspaces`. The ignored-workspace comparison uses it. |
| `--dsh-ignore-workspace` | `autorules` | Repeatable. dsh workspace whose sessions never become cards, because an automated producer runs there. An empty value tracks every workspace. A name equal to `--dsh-workspace` stops the daemon at startup. |
| `--allow-host` | none | Repeatable. Host names accepted besides `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` and `::1`. |
| `--no-notify` | off | Turns off `notify-send` notifications on the daemon's machine. Browser push is set per device in the inbox's Notifications sheet. |

An invalid `--model` or `--thinking` stops the daemon at startup, for example `ExecStart=%h/.local/bin/triage serve --session work --model provider/model-id --thinking high --allow-host my-machine.my-tailnet.ts.net`. Client commands read `TRIAGE_URL`.

## Web inbox

The web UI is served at `/`. Keyboard controls are digits to choose an option; a finding option opens Dispatch with its implied mode. `a` or Enter opens Dispatch preselected to Plan on report cards that offer Plan recommended step. Confirm in the sheet to launch. `o` opens Other text, `n` focuses the note, `i` opens Dispatch with Investigate, `A` preselects Act, `p` preselects Plan, and `P` preselects Plan into. Dispatch has Mode, Engine, Model and Thinking controls, plus Execution model and Execution effort for Plan into. Plan approval offers the same two execution fields on a dsh plan card. `x` acknowledges and closes a card's tmux pane, `r` requests the final report from a reply card that missed its status line, `R` restores the run of an `Unreported:` or `Ended mid-turn:` card in a new window, `s` skips, `l` defers, `v` peeks at the source pane, `t` opens context, `J` jumps to the pane, `c` opens Compose for a new session with Goal, Plan or Plan into mode, `h` opens history, `j` and `k` move through cards, and `?` opens help. On info cards with a pane, Acknowledge & close remains primary. Cards without a recommended step retain their current acknowledge actions.

The top bar and the card share one centered column. The card head shows the card's place in the deck, such as `1 of 7`, beside a rail with one bar per waiting card in deck order. Higher-priority cards get taller bars, and the shown card's bar is blue. A higher-priority arrival moves the view to the deck's top card. An open Later menu or typed answer text on the shown card keeps it in place. Under the title, a row of facts gives the source, directory, pane, kind, priority and waiting time. From 1100px wide, the evidence (Situation, Plan, Task and Result) fills the left column and the Action options fill the right, so the answer stays beside what it answers. When the window is also at least 760px tall, the Action column stays in view while the evidence scrolls. Narrower screens stack the evidence above the Action block, which then sits next to the dock. Plan cards show their plan under Plan, and info cards show their final reply only under Result. Card text renders as Markdown. The evidence blocks and the final reply show headings, lists, tables, quotes, code blocks, emphasis and links, and titles and option text show code and emphasis without links. Raw HTML shows as literal text, only http, https and mailto links become links, each opens in a new tab, and a remote image in the text shows as a link instead of loading. A report card's own images show as thumbnails under Result and open full size in a new tab; the daemon serves only PNG, JPEG, WebP and GIF from the session's store, never SVG or HTML. A body that parses as JSON shows as one code block. The parser is marked, vendored as `ui/marked.js` with its MIT license in `ui/marked.LICENSE.md`, and the inbox builds the elements from its tokens, so card text never passes through `innerHTML`. When an ask's task repeats its title, the card has no separate Task block. Dock buttons show their keys, as do header buttons on screens wider than 800px, and touch screens hide every key hint. On phones the facts drop their labels and run together under the source name, and the four counts fold into a single total that opens them. The text uses Geist and Geist Mono, and the daemon serves both fonts from `ui/fonts/`.


When a card action advances the inbox, the next card enters from the right. Answering a card below the top returns to the top-ranked card from the left.

A card answer or dispatch never opens a window in the session you are working in. A brief goes to its waiting source pane when available; otherwise a new window opens in the daemon's `--session`, default `triage`. The New session sheet can choose another session. Dispatch and finding answers use the daemon's session and the card's cwd. Every new window uses the daemon's `--model` and `--thinking` unless the request overrides them. Setting `--model @default` follows omp's current default; when unset, omp applies its defaults. Windows stay unnamed so tmux keeps `automatic-rename` and the status bar follows omp's pane title. New sessions leave the inbox open, and their asks and final reports arrive as cards.

When several devices use the inbox, every change appears on each client within a second. If another client handles the shown card, the inbox announces `Handled elsewhere` and moves on. The daemon refuses a second answer with 409. An action moves the inbox to the top-ranked waiting card at once while the daemon types the answer in the background. Card actions on one pane run one at a time. If the action then fails, such as a tmux launch error, the daemon returns 422 with the reason. The inbox shows the error in a toast while the card returns to the deck with its draft. Toasts report errors only: a successful answer, skip, copy, jump, reload or session start shows none, and the screen-reader announcer still reads each result.

Long titles clamp to two lines on every card, with a Show more toggle in the header that reveals the rest. Error toasts and history rows clamp long titles the same way. This keeps a card's long title from pushing the actions out of reach or covering the screen. Error toasts sit bottom-center on every viewport.

`tests/ui-checks.js` holds the browser regression checks. Open the inbox, paste the file into the DevTools console or load it with the Chrome MCP `evaluate_script` tool, then call `checkTriage('<scenario>')` and read the result or assertion message. Scenarios are `peek`, `dimensions`, `portrait` and `landscape`, which need the pane peek open and loaded first, plus `offline`, `recovery`, `finding-other`, `compose-stays`, `picker`, `dispatch`, `history-retry`, `long-title`, `mobile-card`, `pwa`, `plan-into`, `plan-approval`, `notify` and `dsh-card`, which run from the inbox view. `long-title` needs the shown card to carry a title longer than two lines, `mobile-card` needs a card with options in a viewport at most 639px wide, and `plan-into` and `plan-approval` need a plan card in the queue. `dsh-card` requires a dsh card in the queue and checks that it offers Open and Peek in place of Jump. `picker` checks the full-screen phone layout at most 639px wide and the dropdown layout above that. `notify` checks that the header fits with the bell and that the Notifications sheet offers Turn on exactly when the device can subscribe.

For remote access, publish the server on a tailnet with `tailscale serve --bg --https=7340 http://127.0.0.1:7340`. That gives `https://<machine>.<tailnet>.ts.net:7340/` with a Tailscale certificate and tailnet-only reach. Start the daemon with `--allow-host <machine>.<tailnet>.ts.net` so it accepts that host name. The published port must match the `--listen` port or be the default HTTPS port. The server has no authentication of its own, so never expose it beyond the tailnet. `tailscale serve --https=7340 off` removes it.

On iPhone and iPad, connect the Tailscale app before opening the inbox. A `ts.net` name resolves only through Tailscale's DNS. Without the tunnel there is nothing to reach. iOS suspends the app in the background. Turning on Connect on Demand in the Tailscale app keeps it running. Some Safari settings reroute DNS around Tailscale. These include iCloud Private Relay, Limit IP Address Tracking, or an encrypted-DNS profile. With any of them on, the name fails to resolve even while the tunnel is up. Try the Home Screen icon first, since it is not a Safari tab and avoids those Safari-only settings.

## Scope and limits

The default address is `http://127.0.0.1:7340`. Triage understands two harnesses, omp in tmux panes and dsh through the dsh web host. Omp's native Plan Review overlay is the first signature in a generic screen-signature → card → keys relay. Claude Code's `--permission-mode plan` and other harnesses can use the same path as future signature rows, without new plumbing. Multi-select asks appear in the inbox but must be answered in their pane; multi-question asks are answered through the inbox step by step. A restored pane that gets the same pane id its reply had before keeps the old card id, which still blocks a re-queue. A report from a session omp resumed under a new pane id after a tmux restart, or one the exit audit queues after its pane is gone, has no Situation or Task.
A pane that vanishes while the daemon is down is never audited, because the tracker's seen-set lives in memory.

## Known Issues

Two performance limits are known. Each poll tick scans and parses every open and deferred card under the database mutex, and the poller runs the full queue query twice per tick. Open behavior limits are the ones under Scope and limits.
